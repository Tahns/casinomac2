Date: 2026-10-26. Angle: what is winning on Stake and in the wider crypto/social slot market in 2025-2026, how rivals structure bonuses, price and name buy features, position volatility, and show max win and RTP; 10 patterns to borrow in spirit, 10 clichés to avoid, and where a 1960s spy-jazz mission slot with several bonus missions and varying grids can stand out.

## 0. Method and limits
- Tools: WebSearch (US-only) and WebFetch (returns a small-model summary of the page, not raw text). Tags: [F] page fetched this session; [S] search-result summary only (page itself blocked or not opened); [R] read in this repo.
- Blocked or empty: stake.com pages and the Stake blog returned 403 to WebFetch, so Stake's own game listings (Mission X, Dragonspire, Le Catcher) are known only from search summaries of the page text. Official Engine docs (stake-engine.com, stakeengine.github.io) were not readable; platform rules below come from the user-supplied self-check in this repo [R] and an unofficial docs mirror [F].
- Popularity evidence is thin. There is no Stake.com ranked play list in the sources. What exists: Stake.us monthly press posts (play volume, no counts), affiliate/review pages, one tracked-lobby study. Treat "popular" as "named by a source", never as measured share.
- Evidence quality: strong = primary page read and specific; medium = specific facts from a reputable review/press page or an operator page seen via search summary; weak = promotional, affiliate, opinion, conflicting figures.
- Figures conflict across sources more often than usual (RTP, max win, grid). Conflicts are shown, not resolved.

## 1. Bottom line
1. Several-bonus structure is table stakes on Stake in 2026, not a differentiator. Scatter-count ladders that open differently named bonuses appear in Le Fisherman (3/4/5 scatters, three modes), Le Catcher (three bonuses), Duck Hunters (3/4/5), Odin's Vault (super/legendary/mythic), Rage of Egypt (two bonuses plus buys). The differentiator must be HOW the missions differ (board shape, rule, tempo), not that there are several.
2. Buy ladders have become a template: a cheap 2-3x "trigger odds" option, a 50-130x entry, a 200-500x premium, plus a volatility switch (Dragonspire, Mission X, Motor Ways, Le Fisherman). Our 5000x cap does not fit a 500x rung (see section 6).
3. A spy slot already exists inside Stake's own exclusive line: Mission X (Uppercut Gaming, "Only on Stake", 14 July 2026 per the listing) with a global multiplier in its bonuses and buy names Super Boost, Hyper Boost, Bonus, Super Bonus, Intel Rush. It is the nearest rival and a distinctness risk under Engine directive [253]. Our look, grid, sound and mission mechanics must be visibly different. Detail in section 4.
4. Market norm for the headline is a big max-win multiple (10,000x is the mode in the sources; 15,000x to 500,000x for high-volatility titles). A 5000x cap is at the low end (Agent Destiny, Fire Stampede 4,275x, Sweet Fiesta 5,966x exist). Do not chase caps. Compete on cadence, clarity and personality, and say 5000x plainly.
5. RTP presentation: single figure plus "house edge" on Stake listings [S]; Hacksaw ships several RTP configs per game (96.33 / 94.33 / 92.34 percent; Demonic Dolls down to 86.21 percent) [F]. A single published RTP, identical across every mode, is a real trust differentiator and the Engine format enforces it for us.
6. Medium and low-medium volatility titles are well represented in Stake.us top lists (Le Fisherman, Le Viking, Samurai Dogs, Candy Crunch, Pixel Cafe), next to high-volatility ones (Rage of Egypt, Tome of Life, Mission X). Extreme volatility is not a precondition for being played on Stake.us (small, press-based sample).
7. Open space: 1960s cut-paper cartoon identity, jazz/mambo music identity, an adult woman lead with deadpan comedy, and per-mission board geometry. None was seen in the Stake-lobby sources read. Absence in a limited scan is not proof of absence.
8. Competition for lobby space is structural: Engine publishers hold 11.5-13.2 percent of Stake's Home shelf in three tracked markets, and Originals plus Engine games hold 8 of the first 10 tracked Home positions there [F, S17]. Several of those studios (Twist, Massive, Paperclip, Uppercut, Knucklehead Syndicate) are Easygo-affiliated and share the Engine base. An indie must win on identity, not on volume.

## 3. Positioning dimensions
### 3.1 Volatility and hit rate
- Le Fisherman publishes hit frequency 42 percent with low-medium volatility and a 4.2/5 expert score, and was the top-played slot on Stake.us in March 2026 [S1, S3]. Our base hit rate is 29.07 percent, and only 8.88 percent of base spins return more than the stake [R MATH_REPORT, 04_attractiveness]. Cadence is a clear gap to the best-played low-volatility lane.
- High-volatility headliners (Rage of Egypt, Mission X, Odin's Vault) are described by reviewers as having long gaps ("can go long stretches without a meaningful win", S13 summary). One Le Catcher reviewer found wins scarce in a session (anecdote, S9).
- Volatility switches (Dragonspire, Mission X, Pixel Cafe) and two-mode games (Ra vs Osiris: Eternity, Pragmatic, week of 13 Oct 2026, S10) show players are offered a choice of risk profile. On Engine this must be separate modes with the same RTP.
- Realized-RTP display: one review lists "all-time" and "last 7 days" RTP per game on Stake.us (Fish 97.03 all-time, Buffalo 90.94, Mother Clucker 93.31, Le Catcher 95.15; S9, weak, not confirmed on a Stake page). If Stake does show realized RTP, high-volatility games will show noisy and sometimes low numbers; a game with more frequent small wins shows steadier ones.

### 3.2 Bonus structures seen
- Scatter-count tiers: 3/4/5 scatters pick the bonus (Le Fisherman, Duck Hunters, Odin's Vault, Le Catcher; Fist of Demolition on 3 and 4). Tier names are story nouns, not "Bonus 1/2/3".
- Same board in every mode is the norm; geometry changes appear only as a per-game identity (Blue Samurai uneven reels, Pixel Cafe 7x7, Mission X 5x4) or as growth inside Hold & Win bonuses (We-Heart, S27 weak; Goal Bonanza 3x5 to 4x5 and Big Bamboo 2 grid growth via a search summary, weak).
- Progress bars: Big Catch Bar (4 levels, one bonus starts at level 4 with no retrigger), Mission X wild count + global multiplier, Pixel Cafe progressive multiplier, Samurai Dogs global multiplier. Our Mission Meter is in line with this and is not novel on its own.
- Hidden random bonus: Mother Clucker (S26, S9). A bonus that triggers without a visible price or odds is the opposite of what Engine [266] and the no-deceptive-design rule allow us to do.

### 3.3 Buy-feature naming and pricing
- Naming families: (a) trigger-odds enhancers ("Extra Chance", "BonusHunt FeatureSpins", "Super Boost", "Hyper Boost"); (b) story-named buys ("Dragon's Call", "Nitro Bonus", "Rainbow Epic Drop", "Intel Rush", "Kill Spin", "Massacre Spin"); (c) plain "Bonus / Super Bonus".
- Price points: 2-3x enhancers; 50-130x entry; 200-300x premium; 500x top rung. Hacksaw lists RTP for each buy (96.14 to 96.33 in Le Fisherman).
- Engine rules that bite names: modes may not be called "BONUS BUY", "BUY FEATURE", "SUPER BET" in social mode; "buy", "purchase", "cost of", "pay" and similar are forbidden words there; any mode costing more than 2x the stake needs a confirm dialog or a clearly priced toggle; 20 modes maximum; titles may not contain Megaways, XWays, Enhanced, Boosted, Rush, Kraken, "Gates of...", "...Bonanza", Olympus [R, 00_ENGINE_SELFCHECK_PROMPT.md]. Ghost Rush (Paperclip) and Intel Rush (Mission X) show that "Rush" is in rivals' vocabulary even though it is a banned title word for us.
- Regulatory context: the UK Gambling Commission warned operators that in-game feature buy-ins do not meet its technical standards, and bans exist or are debated elsewhere (Netherlands blocked; Sweden poll-driven debate) [S23, weak-medium, summary only, dates not retrieved]. OLBG lists Le Fisherman's buys "for players outside the UK" [S1]. The Engine opened to external distribution in September 2026 (search summary only), so buy, turbo and autoplay must stay config-flaggable per jurisdiction.

### 3.4 How max win and RTP are presented
- Max win: a bet multiple ("10,000x") in the first line, usually with no odds. Streamer press frames a hit as a share of the cap ("126,243x = 25.25 percent of the max possible multiplier", Odin's Vault, S12). Sources disagree on cap values for the same game (Gator Hunters 2, Le Fisherman page lists "EUR 750,000" beside 15,000x), which is itself a trust cost.
- RTP: single percentage in a spec table, with "house edge" restated on Stake's listings (Dragonspire and Mission X: 96.00 percent, 4.00 percent) [S15, S16]. Operators of Hacksaw and Nolimit titles can run lower RTP configs; Stake.us pages say players should check the in-game panel [S10].
- Per-mode RTP is shown by Hacksaw (Le Fisherman) and required by Engine [25] and [27] for us.
- Odds of max win: not shown in any source read. A third-party summary of the Engine docs says max-win frequency is typically at least 1 in 10,000,000 and must be obtainable [S29, unverified]; our base max-win odds are 1 in 5,000,002 and 1 in 50,000 in the 100x mode [R MATH_REPORT].

## 5. Ten patterns worth borrowing in spirit (never copying)
Each item: what the market does, our own version, constraint.
1. Scatter-count ladder opening differently named missions (Le Fisherman, Duck Hunters, Odin's Vault, Le Catcher, S1, S11, S12). Spirit: 4-5-6+ scatters select among Mission Briefing variants, each with its own named board and rule. Constraint: every mission explained on the info screen [29]; RTP identical across modes; mission selection by scatter count is chance, not choice.
2. Per-mode price and RTP table (Le Fisherman lists RTP per buy; Stake listing shows RTP + house edge, S1, S15). Spirit: an in-game "mission dossier" screen listing each mode's price, RTP (same), max win, median, share of rounds that return less than the price. Constraint: figures must match math [25][27]; no cherry-picked ratio. Add a "share of the 5000x cap" readout only if it is not used to hype.
3. Cheap odds-enhancer around 2-3x (Extra Chance, BonusHunt, Super Boost; S1, S5, S15, S16). Spirit: one optional, clearly priced, same-RTP mode with honest text ("raises the chance of the mission trigger; also raises the price per play"). Constraint: it is a separate Engine mode; cost over 2x needs confirm or an always-visible priced toggle [31]; social wording; jurisdiction flag; neither a promise of better value nor a nudge.
4. Risk-profile choice as separate same-RTP modes (Dragonspire, Mission X, Pixel Cafe volatility switch; Ra vs Osiris two modes). Spirit: "clearance" levels with plainly stated hit rate and max win per level. Constraint: all modes carry the same RTP; each mode needs its own books; counts toward the 20-mode cap.
5. Persistent visible progress with named levels (Big Catch Bar, Mission X trackers, Pixel Cafe multiplier). Spirit: Mission Meter with named bands carried across missions. Constraint: avoid per-cell persistent multipliers (patent screen in 01_patent_zones.md).
6. Story-flavoured mode names (Dragon's Call, Rainbow Epic Drop, Nitro Bonus). Spirit: spy-trade nouns that describe the mission (no violence, no price words, no Rush/Boost/Super/Intel). Constraint: [225] forbids "BONUS BUY" and similar; trademark search needed for every name.
7. Character-led humour and distinctive animation (Le Viking called "the most fun slot at Stake.us", Tome of Life and Samurai Dogs praised for animation, Blue Samurai described as more elaborately animated; S8, S7, weak opinions). Spirit: Mambo's deadpan reaction beats tied to events in the book; animation made in code. Constraint: changes of outfit must change the rules of the mission, not only the sprite.
8. Non-rectangular or varied geometry as identity (Blue Samurai 3-4-4-4-3, Pixel Cafe 7x7, Mission X 5x4, Dragonspire 5x5). Spirit: each mission has its own board shape, shown on the dossier screen before it starts. Constraint: events must carry the grid; renderer must support each; patent zones for shrinking, expanding and morphing grids are screened in 01_patent_zones.md and 02_variable_grids.md; not cleared.
9. Disclosed in-base surprise events (Fire Stampede Random Grand Reward 4,000x, multiplier orbs; S7). Spirit: a visible "intel drop" in the base game with odds stated in the rules. Constraint: any shown probability must match math [266]; do not copy the hidden bonus of Mother Clucker.
10. Fast, readable play with a published hit rate (Le Fisherman 42 percent; Tome of Life praised as "as easy as it gets for beginners"; Turbo modes in Le Catcher; S1, S7, S9). Spirit: raise base cadence if the optimizer allows, a one-screen rule teach, brisk tumbles. Constraint: speed controls are jurisdiction flags; no celebration of returns at or below the stake (UKGC RTS 14F in 04_attractiveness.md).

## 6. Ten saturated clichés to avoid
1. Egypt, Greek and Norse mythology, Olympus-style gods (Rage of Egypt, Ra vs Osiris, Odin's Vault, Chalice of Olympia, Rise of Asgard).
2. Horror, occult, ghosts, dolls (Demonic Dolls, Ghost Rush, Liliths Curse, Wings of Death 2, Tome of Life, Mental).
3. Candy and "Bonanza/Gates/1000" echo titles (Sweet Bonanza 1000, Sweet Fiesta, Candy Crunch, Gates of Heaven 1000); titles with such words also fail Engine [213].
4. Fishing and the "Le" animal-in-costume franchise look (Le Fisherman, Le Catcher, Le Viking, Le Bunny, Le Cowboy).
5. Wild West and outlaw (Wanted Dead or a Wild, Le Cowboy, Cowboy 1000, Fire Stampede).
6. Samurai and Japanese animals (Samurai Dogs, Samurai 1000, Blue Samurai, Blade Dance, Ore No Konbini).
7. Hold-and-win, link and tiered-jackpot grids (Buffalo H&W Extreme 10,000, Diamonds Power XXL, Xmas Link, Jade Path H&W). Also an Aristocrat-patent area per 01_patent_zones.md.
8. Cap and number inflation in names and marketing ("1000", "2500", "Extreme 10,000", 25,000x to 500,000x). We cap at 5000x; say so without apology.
9. The stacked buy ladder with intensifier nouns (Boost, Hyper, Super, Rush, Extra Chance, Hunt) plus a volatility switch (Mission X, Dragonspire, Le Fisherman, Motor Ways).
10. Generic spy furniture: tuxedo, martini/cocktail, briefcase, spy watch, silhouetted gunman, snowy night skyline, 007 echoes, Pink Panther and Austin Powers echoes [S15, R DESIGN.md].
Also do not borrow (deceptive or opaque): hidden bonus buys with undisclosed price (Mother Clucker), per-operator RTP downgrades (Demonic Dolls 86.21 percent), unexplained "mystery" modes, tease emphasis after a non-trigger, gamble features (Ra vs Osiris gamble to 100x).

Math consequence of copying the buy ladder (computed here from the repo's 96 percent RTP and 5000x cap): the cap is 50x the 100x price; at 250x it is 20x; at 500x it is 10x. A 500x rung would have to be a very low-variance mode (mean about 480x, cap only 10x the price). Keep any second rung at or below roughly 200-250x, or raise the cap (a platform and attorney matter, not a free choice).

## 8. Platform and compliance notes tied to this scan
- Engine format: books chosen by weighted lookup; "player choice" must map to a separate mode (20 modes maximum, RTP identical, payouts in multiples of 0.1x); purely cosmetic choice is allowed [R]. Bet-mode fields: cost, rtp, max_win, auto_close_disabled, is_feature, is_buybonus, which tells the front end to switch assets or apply restrictions [S19, unofficial mirror].
- Directives that shape names and tiers: [31] confirm for any mode over 2x; [225] no forbidden mode names in social mode; [213] title bans; [253] distinctness; [27] each mode described with cost; [25] RTP and max win per mode; [185] general warning text [R].
- Social/Stake.us: tokens are shown as GC and SC (XGC, XSC) and Engine's social word list bans "bonus buy", "buy", "bet", "paytable" and others [R, S20]. Mode names and the dossier screen must be written in that vocabulary from the start.
- Forum signals worth respecting: "95 percent of games are lacklustre", developers "copy the same bonus formats", RTP suspicion; provably fair proves the outcome, not the RTP (Bitcointalk, Aug-Sep 2026, S18, anecdotal). A clear single RTP and per-mode dossier answer these directly.
- Stream-driven discovery: Stake Engine hits are described as streamer-pushed and "Only on Stake" exclusives earn 2x VIP progress (win.gg, forum claim, S11, S18, weak). Not a design lever for an indie, but it explains why clips of one big moment matter: make a single clearly readable peak animation (mission clear, meter cap) rather than constant noise.
